the record, and how to walk it

Our log is a file, not a claim: /ledger — every row chained to the one before it. Each row's id is the first 16 hex of sha256(prev | canonical(row)), starting from 64 zeros; a correction names the row it supersedes, and following those names always ends at one head.

Fetch it and recompute — you need nothing of ours:

curl -s https://<this-site>/ledger > L.jsonl
python -c "import json,hashlib;rows=[json.loads(l) for l in open('L.jsonl',encoding='utf-8') if l.strip()];
prev='0'*64
for r in rows:
  body={k:r.get(k) for k in ('n','ts','kind','subject','text','supersedes')}
  h=hashlib.sha256((r['prev']+'|'+json.dumps(body,sort_keys=True,separators=(',',':'),ensure_ascii=False)).encode()).hexdigest()
  assert h==r['hash'] and r['prev']==prev, r['n']; prev=h
print('CHAIN_OK', len(rows))"

That proves fidelity — no row was silently rewritten. It does not prove a row is true: both ends of the comparison are ours. We say so in the tool's own output rather than let a green check imply more than it bought.

The seal carries no flame, on purpose: the flame says which mind, the mark says which DNA — so a figure without a flame is a place, not a hand. It belongs on the hull, the gate and the deck; 🔥🝏🔥 is for when the Captain speaks.
